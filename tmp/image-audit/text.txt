# **FG-01 \-- 001 \-- Register Account**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-001 — Register Account

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A Guest requests an email/password account and completes the required email-verification phase. Hza supplies the registration and check-email screens; the verification transport must be reconciled with the earlier Backend specification.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Guest (G)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The Guest selects Register from the sign-in page.

   2\. The System displays optional display name, email, password and password confirmation fields.

   3\. The Guest enters the information and selects Register.

   4\. The System validates the fields and email availability and records a pending registration.

   5\. The System requests verification delivery and displays the check-email page through UC-004.

   6\. The Guest completes the supported verification action. The System revalidates the pending request, email availability and single-use verification evidence.

   7\. The System creates the verified account and authenticated session only after confirmed completion, then returns to a safe shopping destination.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 4, email is malformed, required fields are missing, passwords differ or the password fails policy: show field validation and create no completed account.

   A2. At steps 4 or 6, the email is already in use: refuse duplicate registration and offer sign-in/recovery without exposing private account details.

   A3. At step 6, verification is expired, incorrect, reused or superseded: reject completion; UC-004 handles resend.

   A4. Before step 6, the Guest leaves: no completed account/session is reported.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. At step 5, email delivery fails: offer a controlled retry/resend and do not claim receipt.

   A6. At step 6, concurrent registration claims the same email: uniqueness validation rejects the second completion.

   A7. At steps 4–7, persistence fails or the result is uncertain: reconcile account/pending state before retry and do not create duplicate accounts.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Hza marks display name optional and password minimum eight characters. Do not invent uppercase/symbol requirements.

   • The 11 November source describes six-digit codes valid ten minutes, pending-request information and a password supplied again at confirmation. Hza instead instructs opening a verification link. The token/code contract and password handoff require team resolution; no code-entry screen is supplied.

   • Store only password hashes; never log password or verification secrets.

   • Preserve a safe selected-cart/navigation context without merging another user’s private cart.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • Registration is enabled and the Guest can access the mailbox used.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • One verified account and a confirmed session exist; pending verification evidence is consumed.

   Failure:

   • No unsuccessful attempt is labeled a completed account or authenticated session.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   No named UML extension location is verified in the supplied model tab. Related use cases: UC-002 Login; UC-004 Verify Email; UC-007 Login with Google.

   Scope: Replaces the old Register Account file under canonical UC-001. Hza is the UI source; earlier Backend behavior is a dated specification, not independently verified implementation evidence.

# **FG-01 \-- 002 \-- Login**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-002 — Login

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A Guest signs in with an eligible local-password account and returns to the appropriate storefront or administrator destination.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Guest (G)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The Guest opens Login, optionally from a protected shopping action.

   2\. The System displays email and password inputs with recovery and Google sign-in entry points.

   3\. The Guest enters credentials and submits.

   4\. The System validates the request, finds the account and checks its password and login eligibility.

   5\. The System creates a new session and returns authentication information only after successful validation.

   6\. The Client establishes the session and navigates to an authorized safe destination while preserving permitted shopping context.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 4, fields are incomplete/malformed: identify input errors without creating a session.

   A2. At step 4, the identifier is unknown or the password is wrong: show the same generic authentication failure.

   A3. At step 4, an account is inactive/locked or otherwise ineligible: refuse login; do not bypass account restrictions.

   A4. At step 2, the Guest selects Forgot Password or Google: continue through UC-005 or UC-007.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. At step 4, repeated attempts exceed the configured rate policy: throttle and offer retry after the permitted interval; no numeric limit is established here.

   A6. At steps 4–5, authentication/network service fails: show a recoverable unconfirmed state and retain safe context, not a false login success.

   A7. At step 6, a return URL is unsafe or unauthorized: use the role-appropriate default destination.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Hza labels the input Email; the earlier Backend contract calls it identifier. Map the UI to the agreed contract without claiming username-only login.

   • The prior source specifies access/refresh tokens. Their secure storage, expiry and rotation contract must be confirmed before implementation.

   • Remember Me appears in the desktop design, but its duration and persistence policy are unspecified.

   • Do not expose account existence or credentials through errors or logs.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • A local-password account exists and is eligible for a successful sign-in.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • A new authorized session exists and the actor reaches an allowed destination.

   Failure:

   • Rejected credentials do not create a new session or disclose private data.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   No named UML extension location is verified in the supplied model tab. Related use cases: UC-005 Reset Password; UC-007 Login with Google; UC-003 Logout.

   Scope: Hza includes desktop/mobile login and a generic failure screen. Administrator role routing is a requirement from the actor model; Hza does not supply a separate administrator login screen.

# **FG-01 \-- 003 \-- Logout**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-003 — Logout

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A signed-in actor ends the current session and returns to a signed-out interface without terminating unrelated device sessions.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Customer (C), Administrator (A)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The actor selects Logout from account navigation.

   2\. The System presents the confirmation shown in Hza.

   3\. The actor confirms logout.

   4\. The System identifies the intended session from validated session/refresh evidence and revokes that session.

   5\. The Client clears current-session authentication state and displays the confirmed signed-out page.

   6\. The actor may browse public products or sign in again.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 3, the actor cancels: retain the current authenticated state.

   A2. At step 4, the session is already expired/revoked: clear local authentication and show the applicable signed-out outcome without revoking another session.

   A3. At step 4, the token cannot identify a session: do not revoke an arbitrary account/session.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A4. At step 4, the network fails: clear local authentication for this client and state that server revocation is unconfirmed; do not promise all access tokens have been invalidated.

   A5. After logout, a protected request is made: enforce authentication again.

   A6. Other active device sessions remain active unless separately revoked under UC-008.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • The prior Backend source limits logout to the session identified by the refresh token.

   • The access-token invalidation mechanism must match the server’s revocation/expiry policy; UI cleanup alone is not proof of server revocation.

   • Account/order data are retained. Administrator access is protected after logout.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • The actor has a current or locally remembered session to terminate.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • The identified session is revoked, local credentials are cleared and signed-out state is shown.

   Failure:

   • No unrelated session is revoked; an uncertain server outcome is not reported as confirmed.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   No named UML extension location is verified in the supplied model tab. Related use cases: UC-002 Login; UC-008 Manage Active Sessions.

   Scope: Hza provides confirm-logout and signed-out screens for the storefront. Equivalent administrator navigation is not separately shown.

   Sources: Vision v1.4; shared PA3 Use-Case Model; Hza.zip static exports; earlier Thành specifications dated 11 November 2026\. Prior Backend statements have not been revalidated against repository code in this revision.

# **FG-01 \-- 004 \-- Verify Email**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-004 — Verify Email

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A Guest proves control of the registration mailbox and completes a pending email/password registration. Optional is the model priority; the earlier registration specification requires verification for this registration path.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Guest (G)

   Priority: Optional

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. UC-001 creates a pending registration and requests verification delivery.

   2\. The System displays the Hza check-email instructions and resend/continue actions.

   3\. The Guest opens the verification message and performs its supported confirmation action.

   4\. The System validates pending-request identity, evidence integrity, expiry, latest issuance and unused state.

   5\. The System rechecks email uniqueness and completes the verified account/session atomically with consumption of verification evidence.

   6\. The Guest selects Continue; the System verifies the real completion state before returning to shopping.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 4, evidence is invalid, expired, used or superseded: reject confirmation and offer resend.

   A2. At step 2, the Guest requests resend: apply rate controls, replace prior evidence and display the delivery outcome.

   A3. At step 6, the Guest has not verified: keep the check-email state; clicking Continue must not verify the account.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A4. At step 5, the email has become unavailable: reject duplicate-account completion.

   A5. At steps 1–3, delivery is delayed/unavailable: show retry guidance without claiming email receipt.

   A6. At step 5, persistence fails or a repeated callback arrives: reconcile the result and do not create a second account/session through replay.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Design discrepancy: Hza describes an email link; the earlier source requires a six-digit code, ten-minute expiry, pending-request information and password at confirmation. These are alternative contracts requiring approval, not simultaneous UI steps.

   • Resending invalidates earlier evidence; secrets must be single-use and excluded from logs.

   • This Guest registration flow is separate from the signed-in change-email subflow in UC-009.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • A pending registration exists; verification delivery is enabled.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • The verified account/session is created once and verification evidence is consumed.

   Failure:

   • Failed verification does not create a verified account or disclose another registration.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   No named UML extension location is verified in the supplied model tab. Related use cases: UC-001 Register Account; UC-009 Manage Profile.

   Scope: Canonical UC-004 retains the model’s Optional priority while documenting mandatory verification in the earlier registration flow. Hza has no code-entry or verification-result screen.

# **FG-01 \-- 005 \-- Reset Password**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-005 — Reset Password

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A user who cannot use the local password requests a recovery link and sets a new password after proving control of valid recovery evidence.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Guest (G), Customer (C)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The actor selects Forgot Password from Login.

   2\. The System displays the recovery-email form.

   3\. The actor submits an email; the System validates its format and presents the same neutral check-email response for eligible and unknown accounts.

   4\. Where recovery is eligible, the System sends a bounded, single-use recovery link.

   5\. The actor opens a valid link and enters/ confirms a new password on the reset form.

   6\. The System validates the recovery evidence and password, stores the new hash and consumes the evidence consistently.

   7\. The System confirms the password change and offers Login.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 3, email format is invalid: show input validation.

   A2. At step 3, no eligible local-password account matches: keep the neutral delivery response and do not expose account existence.

   A3. At step 5, the link is expired, invalid or consumed: display the Hza expired-link state and offer a new request.

   A4. At step 6, the password is shorter than eight characters or confirmation differs: reject and preserve the form context safely.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. Before step 6, the actor cancels: do not change the stored password.

   A6. At step 4, delivery fails or requests are throttled: show safe retry guidance.

   A7. At step 6, persistence fails/response is uncertain: do not claim success; reconcile evidence consumption before retry.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Hza supplies forgot/reset/check-email/expired-link/password-changed screens and specifies at least eight characters.

   • Reset-token lifetime, eligibility of Google-only accounts and post-reset session-revocation policy are open decisions; do not invent their implementation.

   • Recommended acceptance rule for approval: revoke existing sessions after a successful recovery and require fresh login.

   • Never expose the password or recovery token in UI diagnostics or logs.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • The actor can access the recovery mailbox; the recovery service is enabled.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • The password hash is updated once and the recovery evidence cannot be replayed.

   Failure:

   • Rejected recovery leaves the password unchanged and does not disclose account existence.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   No named UML extension location is verified in the supplied model tab. Related use cases: UC-002 Login; UC-006 Change Password.

   Scope: UC-005 was missing/misnumbered in the old Thành table. This specification derives the UI flow from Hza; no recovery implementation was verified from source code.

# **FG-01 \-- 006 \-- Change Password**

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-006 — Change Password

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   An authenticated Customer with an existing local password changes it after proving the current password and signs in again after session revocation.

   Functional group: FG-01 — Authentication

   Actor(s): Customer (C)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The Customer opens Change Password from the profile.

   2\. The System verifies the session and local-password eligibility and displays current/new/confirmation inputs.

   3\. The Customer enters the values and selects Save New Password.

   4\. The System checks the current password, new-password policy, confirmation and non-reuse.

   5\. The System stores the new password hash and revokes all account sessions as specified by the earlier Backend document.

   6\. The System reports the confirmed change and requires fresh login.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 2, the account has no local password: offer the separately approved initial-password path within UC-009 rather than requesting a nonexistent current password.

   A2. At step 4, the current password is wrong: reject without changing the password.

   A3. At step 4, the new password equals the old one, fails policy or differs from confirmation: show validation.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A4. At steps 2 or 5, the session is invalid or account inactive: deny the change.

   A5. Before step 3, the Customer cancels: retain the existing password.

   A6. At step 5, persistence/session revocation fails or the outcome is uncertain: report an unconfirmed result and reconcile; do not promise revocation without evidence.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Hza specifies minimum eight characters. The earlier Backend specification adds maximum 72 UTF-8 bytes, not 72 characters; confirm that constraint against the final password-hashing contract.

   • Do not invent mandatory uppercase, lowercase, digits or symbols.

   UC-014 — Filter & Sort Products

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A Guest or Customer narrows a product catalog or search-result list by applying supported product attributes and sorting options. The System returns only published products that satisfy the selected criteria and clearly reports when no products match.

   Functional group: FG-03 — Search and Browse

   Actor(s): Guest (G), Customer (C)

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The Guest or Customer opens a category listing or a product-search result list.

   2\. The System displays the available filter controls for the current product category, the current sorting option, and any filters already applied.

   3\. The Guest or Customer selects one or more supported criteria. The Hza category design shows brand, price range, usage needs, minimum RAM, chipset/CPU, and screen-size controls.

   4\. The Guest or Customer selects Apply Filters.

   5\. The System validates the selected criteria, including the price range, and submits the filter request with the current category and search context.

   6\. The System retrieves published products that satisfy the criteria and applies the selected supported sort order, such as popularity or price where available.

   7\. The System displays the matching product cards, result count, applied-filter chips, clear-all action, and pagination controls.

   8\. The Guest or Customer may change the sort order, remove an individual filter, clear all filters, or open a product through UC-015 View Product Details.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 3 or 5, the price range is invalid (for example, the minimum exceeds the maximum or a value is outside the accepted range): the System displays a validation message and does not replace the last valid result set.

   A2. At step 6, no products satisfy the selected criteria: the System displays an empty-results state, identifies the active criteria, and offers a clear-all action or a way to relax the filters. The Hza export includes this state.

   A3. A selected attribute is not supported for the current category: the System rejects or ignores the unsupported criterion according to the approved filter contract and must not return products as though that criterion had been applied.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A4. Catalog data changes while the filter request is being processed: the System displays the latest validated result set and does not include unpublished products.

   A5. The filter/search service fails or times out: the System displays a recoverable error and retry action, preserves the actor's selected criteria, and does not fabricate results or counts.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Filter options must be category-aware and based on supported catalog attributes; missing attributes must not be inferred.

   • The System must apply all selected criteria consistently and display the active criteria so the actor can understand why products are included.

   • Price values must be validated and displayed in VND. The exact inclusive/exclusive boundary rules for price ranges must be consistent across Frontend and Backend.

   • Sorting options must be explicit and deterministic. The current Hza design visibly shows “Phổ biến nhất”; the complete sort-option list requires confirmation with Backend.

   • Result counts and pagination must reflect the same active filter and sort state. Clearing filters must remove all active criteria.

   • Product cards and filter values shown in Hza.zip are sample content, not proof of live catalog data or completed implementation.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • The storefront and the relevant category or search-result context are reachable.

   • Catalog attributes and filter/search services are available, or the System can return a defined empty/error state.

   • No authenticated session is required to filter public catalog products.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • A result list matching the valid selected criteria and sort order is displayed.

   • The current filter and sort state is visible and can be changed or cleared.

   Failure:

   • Invalid or unsupported criteria do not silently produce misleading results.

   • No unpublished product or unauthorized catalog data is exposed, and no persistent business data is modified.

   3\. The Guest or Customer selects another product from the same category.

   4\. The System validates that the selected products belong to the same category and that the comparison contains no more than three products.

   5\. When at least two valid products are selected, the Guest or Customer opens the comparison view.

   6\. The System retrieves current product and variant attributes and displays the selected products in side-by-side columns or cards.

   7\. The System aligns comparable attributes, such as price, processor/chipset, memory, storage, display, battery, and other category-relevant specifications. Missing attributes are marked as unavailable.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   • The comparison view should emphasize factual differences and must not claim that one product is universally best when suitability depends on customer priorities.

# **FG-04 \-- 023 \-- Manage Shopping Cart** 

1. # **Use-Case Name** 

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   A logged-in customer collects products in a cart before buying. The customer adds products with a chosen variant, changes quantities, removes items, and selects which items to check out. The system saves the cart to the customer's account, blocks quantities above available stock, and shows each item's price, the subtotal, and the running total. 

   Primary actor: Customer.

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

1. The customer selects the cart icon in the header.  
2. The system loads the customer's saved cart and shows, for each item: image, product name, chosen variant (such as color or storage), unit price, quantity, line total, stock status, and a checkbox to select it for checkout.  
3. The system shows the subtotal and running total of the selected items. Delivery fees and vouchers are applied later, at checkout.  
4. The customer adjusts the cart as needed: changes an item's quantity (2.2.2), removes an item (2.2.3), or selects and deselects items for checkout (2.2.4).  
5. After each change, the system validates it against available stock, saves the cart to the customer's account, and refreshes the line totals, subtotal, and header cart count.  
6. The customer selects Checkout.  
7. The system confirms that at least one item is selected (2.2.8) and that every selected item is still available in the requested quantity (2.2.6).  
8. The system passes the selected items to the Checkout use case. The use case ends.  
   

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Add Product to Cart*

Starts instead of Basic Flow step 1, when the customer is on a product details page or a product listing.

1. The customer chooses a variant if the product has one, then selects Add to cart.  
2. The system checks that a variant is chosen, that the product is active, and that the variant is in stock. If no variant is chosen, the system asks the customer to pick one and adds nothing.  
3. If the same product and variant is already in the cart, the system increases its quantity by the chosen amount (1 by default). Otherwise it adds a new line, selected for checkout by default. If the new quantity would exceed stock, 2.2.5 applies.  
4. The system saves the cart, updates the header cart count, and shows a confirmation message.  
5. The customer keeps browsing, or opens the cart (Basic Flow step 1).  
   .

   2. ### *Change Item Quantity*

Start at Basic Flow step 4\.

1. The customer types a new quantity or uses the plus and minus controls on an item.  
2. The system checks that the quantity is a whole number of at least 1 and not above available stock. If the input is blank, zero, or not a number, the system restores the previous quantity and shows a message. If it is above stock, 2.2.5 applies.  
3. The system saves the quantity and updates the line total, subtotal, and header count.  
4. Resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   3. ### *Change Item Quantity*

Start at Basic Flow step 4\.

1. The customer selects Remove on an item.  
2. The system deletes the item, saves the cart, and updates the subtotal and header count.  
3. If it was the last item, 2.2.7 applies. Otherwise resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   4. ### *Select Items for Checkout*

Start at Basic Flow step 4\.

1. The customer ticks or unticks an item's checkbox, or uses select all.  
2. The system saves the selection and recalculates the subtotal from the selected items only. An item that is out of stock cannot be selected.  
3. Resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   5. ### *Requested Quantity Exceeds Stock*

Starts at step 3 of 2.2.1 or step 2 of 2.2.2.

1. The system rejects the change and keeps the previous quantity.  
2. The system shows the available stock, for example "Only 3 left in stock".  
3. The customer chooses a smaller quantity. Resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   6. ### *Item No Longer Available*

Starts at Basic Flow step 2 or 7, when a product in the cart was deactivated, sold out, or now has less stock than the cart quantity.

1. The system marks the item as unavailable (or shows how many are left) and clears its selection.  
2. The system leaves the item out of the subtotal and does not continue to checkout.  
3. The customer removes the item or lowers its quantity. Resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   7. ### *Cart Is Empty*

Starts at Basic Flow step 2, or after the last item is removed.

1. The system shows an empty-cart message with a link back to the homepage, and disables Checkout.  
2. The use case ends.

   8. ### *No Items Selected*

Starts at Basic Flow step 7\.

1. The system does not continue and asks the customer to select at least one item.  
2. Resume at Basic Flow step 4\.

   

3. # **Special Requirements**

   

- ## Stock check on the server: every add and every quantity change is checked against current stock on the server, not only in the browser.

- ## Persistence: the cart belongs to the customer's account and is still there after logout and on another device or browser.

- ## Current prices: the cart shows the current catalog price. If a price changed since the item was added, the system shows the new price with a notice.

- ## Response time: cart changes should refresh within 2 seconds under normal load.

4. # **Preconditions**

   

- ## The customer is logged in with a customer account.

- ## For 2.2.1 only: the product is active and the chosen variant is in stock.

5. # **Postconditions**

- The cart saved to the customer's account matches the last confirmed items, quantities, and selection, and the header cart count matches it  
- If the customer continued, the selected items and quantities are handed to Checkout. The cart is not emptied by this use case.  
-  If a change was rejected or a system error occurred, the cart stays as last saved.


6. # **Extension Points** {#extension-points}

   

Proceed to Checkout: between Basic Flow steps 7 and 8, the Checkout use case continues from the selected items.

1. # **Use-Case Name**

   UC-050 — Moderate Product Reviews

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   An authorized Administrator examines customer reviews and approves eligible feedback for public display or hides policy-violating content with a reason, retaining the moderation history.

   Functional group: FG-09 — Store Administration

   Actor(s): Administrator (A)

   Priority: Optional

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow**

   1\. The Administrator opens Reviews in the administration interface.

   2\. The System checks authorization and displays paginated reviews with pending, approved and hidden status summaries.

   3\. The Administrator searches by content/customer/product or filters by status, then selects a review.

   4\. The System displays the selected rating, text, customer/product context, purchase verification and current moderation state.

   5\. The Administrator checks the content against the moderation policy and selects Approve for acceptable feedback.

   6\. The System validates permissions and the current review version, records approval with actor/time and updates its public visibility.

   7\. The System confirms the new state and refreshes the moderation list and relevant public rating aggregates.

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. At step 5, content is spam, abusive, irrelevant or exposes personal information: the Administrator selects Hide and supplies a reason; the System preserves the content/history and removes it from public display after a confirmed save.

   A2. At step 5, Hide is selected without a required reason: show validation and retain the current state.

   A3. At step 3, no reviews match: display an empty state with a clear-filter action.

   A4. At steps 4 or 6, the review has been removed or changed by another moderator: reload its status and require a fresh decision.

   A5. Before confirmation, the Administrator cancels: retain the existing visibility.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A6. At steps 2, 4 or 6, authentication expires or privileges are insufficient: deny the request without exposing restricted notes.

   A7. At step 6, saving fails or the response is uncertain: show an unconfirmed result and reconcile current state before retry.

   A8. A permanent delete is requested: the Hza UI provides Hide rather than permanent deletion. Vision permits deletion, but its retention/authorization policy and confirmation UI require approval before enabling it.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Never hide a review merely because its rating is low; Hza restricts hiding to violations, spam or personal-data exposure.

   • Keep moderation reasons internal and preserve an audit record of state transitions.

   • Public readers must not retrieve hidden or pending text through another API path.

   • Compute rating summaries from the same approved visibility rule used by UC-045; refresh/invalidate affected aggregates after moderation.

   • Do not alter a Customer’s rating or text while approving/hiding it.

4. # **Preconditions**

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • The Administrator is authenticated and authorized to moderate reviews.

   • A moderation policy defines publishable content and required reasons.

5. # **Postconditions**

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • The approved/hidden state is persisted, audited and reflected consistently in public review visibility and aggregates.

   Failure:

   • Rejected or uncertain actions are not reported as committed; no unauthorized content or notes are exposed.

6. # **Extension Points**

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope**

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   The Customer asks the Agent to compare two or three electronics products from compatible categories using normalized, verified specifications and plain-language trade-offs.

   Functional group: FG-10 — AI Chatbot & Shopping Automation

   Actor(s): Customer (C); AI Shopping Agent (supporting system); Backend catalog/order services as applicable

   Priority: Core

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

   1\. The Customer selects two or three products or provides their names/IDs in chat.

   2\. The Agent resolves each candidate to a stable Backend product ID, asking the Customer to clarify duplicate names.

   3\. The System validates category compatibility and retrieves fresh authoritative attributes for each product.

   4\. The Agent builds a side-by-side comparison for shared attributes such as price, RAM, storage, display, battery, and availability.

   5\. For missing values, the Agent labels data unavailable and avoids guessing.

   6\. The Agent explains relative strengths based on the Customer's stated goals and budget.

   7\. The Customer opens linked product details or revises the comparison set.

   

   

   

   

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. An ambiguous model name maps to multiple variants: ask for specific variant rather than silently selecting one.

   A2. Requested products are from incompatible categories: explain the limitation and offer narrower same-category comparison.

         1. #### State and authorization conditions

   A3. One product is missing/inactive: request a replacement and do not invent values.

   A4. Specs have inconsistent units: normalize only with verified mapping; otherwise show raw values with a warning.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. The Customer selects more than three products: ask to narrow the set.

   A6. Backend timeout: show temporary unavailability and conventional comparison links if present.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Compare at most three products in this planned UX unless team approves a different bound.

   • Keep units, currencies, and product variants explicit.

   • No universal 'best' claim without user-specific evaluation criteria; preserve missing-value transparency.

   

 


4. # **Preconditions**

   

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • Products are identifiable in the real catalog.

   • The Agent can retrieve approved product details/specification values for each candidate.

5. # **Postconditions**

   

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • A verified comparison and its product links are shown.

   

   Failure:

   • No ungrounded, cross-category, or unauthorized product claim is asserted.

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope** {#related-use-cases-and-scope}

# **FG-10 \-- 056 \-- Manage Shopping Cart via AI** 

1. # **Use-Case Name** 

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   The Customer may ask the Agent to add, remove, or change the quantity of a specific product variant. The Agent must propose the exact cart mutation and obtain an explicit customer confirmation before a Backend-authorized change.

   Functional group: FG-10 — AI Chatbot & Shopping Automation

   Actor(s): Customer (C); AI Shopping Agent (supporting system); Backend catalog/order services as applicable

   Priority: Core in diagram; deferred

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

   1\. The Customer requests to add or change a specified product variant in the chat.

   2\. The Agent retrieves the authoritative product/variant, current stock, and the Customer's own cart.

   3\. The System shows a structured preview: variant ID, product name, requested quantity, current availability, and intended action.

   4\. The Customer explicitly confirms the requested mutation.

   5\. The Backend rechecks authorization, variant status, quantity, and stock before the cart API call.

   6\. The Backend returns the committed cart state or a structured failure.

   7\. The Agent displays the verified updated cart and a link to the normal cart page.

   

   

   

   

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. Customer refuses/cancels the confirmation: no mutation is sent.

   A2. The variant/quantity is ambiguous: request clarification; do not choose silently.

         1. #### State and authorization conditions

   A3. Insufficient stock or changed price/availability: refresh the preview and require a new confirmation.

   A4. Session expired or cart belongs to someone else: reject and request proper authentication.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. Tool is unavailable or action is outside MVP: state that the feature is disabled and link to regular cart controls.

   A6. Timeout/uncertain outcome: re-read cart from Backend and do not automatically retry potentially duplicate mutation.

   A7. Confirmation expired: ask for new confirmation after revalidation; do not use an assumed five-minute expiry as approved policy.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • All cart-changing tool calls require user approval and Backend authorization.

   • The AI must not access the cart database directly or bypass normal quantity/stock rules.

   • The confirmation expiry window and API contract require explicit product/team decisions.

   • Do not falsely mark this optional tool as implemented in the PA3 demo.

 


4. # **Preconditions**

   

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • The Customer is logged in; an authenticated cart tool is implemented and enabled.

   • Variant identity, stock, price, and confirmation policy are known and revalidated at execution.

5. # **Postconditions**

   

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • Only a confirmed, valid Backend cart mutation changes persisted state.

   

   Failure:

   • No mutation occurs on denied/expired/failed confirmation or when feature is unavailable.

6. # **Extension Points** {#extension-points}

   

   1. ## **Related use cases and scope** {#related-use-cases-and-scope}

   No formal UML extension point is defined. No named \<\<extend\>\> location is defined in the current shared use-case model. Related: UC-023 Manage Shopping Cart; optional extension of UC-052 Chat; UC-034 Variants; UC-037 Stock.

# **FG-10 \-- 057 \-- Prepare Checkout via AI** 

1. # **Use-Case Name** 

   1. ## **Brief Description**

   The Agent helps the Customer inspect cart readiness and shipping information or navigate to checkout; final order placement and payment remain exclusively under explicit Customer control.

   Functional group: FG-10 — AI Chatbot & Shopping Automation

   Actor(s): Customer (C); AI Shopping Agent (supporting system); Backend catalog/order services as applicable

   Priority: Core in diagram; deferred

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

   1\. The Customer asks the Agent to prepare checkout for selected cart items.

   2\. The Agent retrieves the owned cart and eligible variants using authorized Backend tools.

   3\. The Agent summarizes selected items, quantities, price/stock freshness, and missing delivery data.

   4\. The System directs the Customer to the normal checkout UI to select/verify address and payment method.

   5\. The Backend validates current stock, address ownership, and totals at checkout.

   6\. The Customer reviews the official checkout form and independently initiates checkout using its own confirmation control.

   7\. The Agent reports only observed status; it does not claim payment or order success without Backend evidence.

   

   

   

   

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

   A1. Cart is empty, products invalid, or stock insufficient: explain which item needs action.

   A2. Shipping address is missing/does not belong to the Customer: direct to secure address editor.

         1. #### State and authorization conditions

   A3. Price or stock changes before checkout: require updated authoritative review.

   A4. AI tool unavailable or feature deferred: provide conventional checkout/cart link without pretending a preview was executed.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   A5. The Customer asks the Agent to confirm order or pay autonomously: refuse that execution path and require standard checkout/payment controls.

   A6. Expired session or API timeout: do not expose cart contents or claim an order was created.

3. # **Special Requirements**

   

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • Checkout in current Backend-SWE directly creates an order (UC-025); there is no implemented separate checkout-preview endpoint.

   • Do not incorrectly document mock AI preparation as order placement or payment.

   • Payment credentials and order confirmation must never be delegated to an unverified AI-only conversation.

   

 


4. # **Preconditions**

   

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • Customer is authenticated and has an accessible cart.

   • Only approved read-only/preparation tools are enabled unless stronger permissions and confirmations have been implemented.

5. # **Postconditions**

   

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • The Customer reaches an accurately represented regular checkout flow when enabled.

   

   Failure:

   • No unauthorized order/payment mutation or unverified checkout success occurs.

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

   1\. The Customer requests status for a specific order or their latest order.

   2\. The Agent prompts for an order identifier if the target is ambiguous.

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

5. # **Postconditions**

   

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Functional group: FG-10 — AI Chatbot & Shopping Automation

   Actor(s): Customer (C); AI Shopping Agent (supporting system); Backend catalog/order services as applicable

   Priority: Optional

2. # **Flow of Events**

   1. ## **Basic Flow** 

   1\. The Customer opens the Chatbox history panel.

   2\. The System retrieves only conversations owned by the current Customer or supported local session.

   3\. The System displays conversation titles, last-updated dates, and available deletion controls.

   4\. The Customer chooses a conversation to view its stored messages.

   5\. The System reloads the selected transcript without asserting stale product prices as current.

   6\. The Customer optionally deletes a conversation and confirms the deletion.

   7\. The System persists removal per policy and updates the history list.

   

   

   

   

   2. ## **Alternative Flows**

      1. ### *Input validation and business-rule alternatives*

      2. ### *Additional outcomes and service failures*

   1. ## **Use-case quality and integrity constraints**

   • A retention period must be approved; do not invent storage duration or automatic memory.

   • Never reuse historical product prices/stock as live catalog facts; refresh where relevant.

   • Stored conversations must enforce ownership, access checks, and safe deletion behavior.

   

 


4. # **Preconditions**

   

   1. ## **Conditions that must hold**

   • Chat-history persistence is enabled with a documented retention and consent policy.

   • Customer is authenticated for account-synced history; session-only history is appropriately isolated.

5. # **Postconditions**

   

   1. ## **Success and failure states**

   Success:

   • An authorized conversation is shown or deletion is confirmed by storage.

   

   Failure:

   • No unauthorized read, fake deletion confirmation, or unconsented persistence occurs.
